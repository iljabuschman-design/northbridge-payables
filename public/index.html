Scope & accounting assumptions

This is a demonstration application built around one fictional company, Northbridge Trading Ltd, with dummy suppliers and transactions. No real company data, live payment rails, bank feeds or HMRC integration are used.

Base currency

The company's base (reporting) currency is GBP. Every invoice and payment carries its own transaction currency (GBP, EUR or USD) plus the exchange rate used to translate it into GBP at that moment. All ledger entries are posted in GBP so the books always speak one currency, while original-currency amounts and rates are retained alongside each entry for reference.

Exchange rates

Rates are entered by the user at the point of recording each invoice or payment, representing "the rate actually used" for that transaction — there is no live FX feed. This matches the brief's requirement to record the exchange rate used, rather than have the system source it automatically. Suggested/last-used rates are offered as a convenience only.

Foreign-exchange gain/loss

A foreign-currency invoice is booked to Accounts Payable at the rate ruling on the invoice date. When it is paid (in full or in part), the portion of the liability being settled is relieved at that original booked rate, while the cash/bank leg is recorded at the rate ruling on the payment date. Any difference between the two is posted as a realised FX gain or loss. This is the standard treatment for monetary payables denominated in a foreign currency and is what lets the app show a gain when the foreign currency has weakened against GBP since the invoice was raised, and a loss when it has strengthened.

The application does not currently perform period-end revaluation of unrealised FX movements on invoices that remain open at a reporting date — only gains/losses realised on actual payment are posted. This would be a natural next enhancement (see below).

VAT

VAT entered on an invoice is treated as recoverable input VAT and posted to a VAT Recoverable account — appropriate for a VAT-registered UK company purchasing taxable supplies. No output VAT, VAT return, partial exemption or reverse-charge mechanics are modelled; the seed data simply uses VAT = 0 for the overseas EUR/USD supplier invoices (treated as zero-rated/reverse-charged in this simplified model) and a normal 20% VAT charge for the UK supplier invoices, to keep the worked example realistic without building a full VAT engine.

Accounts used

Purchases (expense), VAT Recoverable (asset), Accounts Payable (liability, sub-ledgered by invoice), one Bank account per currency (GBP/EUR/USD — reflecting a company that settles suppliers directly from matching-currency accounts), and Realised FX Gain / Realised FX Loss (P&L). Every invoice and payment posts a balanced double-entry journal; the Ledger tab's trial balance should always net to zero, which is checked live on the Dashboard as a sanity check on the books.

Persistence

All data is stored server-side in a SQLite database file, not in the browser, so it survives page refreshes and is visible from any browser hitting this URL.

Ideas for a fuller product

Given more time, natural next steps include: period-end revaluation of open foreign-currency balances at a closing rate (unrealised FX to a separate reserve); supplier statements and an AP ageing report; multi-user roles and an audit trail of edits; approval workflow before an invoice is posted; a live/pluggable FX rate source with the manual rate kept as an override; multi-entity/multi-book consolidation; and export of the ledger to CSV/PDF for the accountant.
