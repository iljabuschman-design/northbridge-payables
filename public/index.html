Scope & accounting assumptions

This is a demonstration application built around one fictional company, Northbridge Trading Ltd, with dummy customers, suppliers and transactions. No real company data, live payment rails or HMRC integration are used.

Base currency

The company's base (reporting) currency is GBP. Every invoice, payment and journal carries its own transaction currency (GBP, EUR or USD) plus the exchange rate used to translate it into GBP. All ledger entries are posted in GBP so the books speak one currency; original-currency amounts and rates are kept alongside each entry for reference.

Exchange rates

Rates come from the European Central Bank's daily reference rates (data.ecb.europa.eu). The ECB quotes everything against the euro, so GBP rates are derived: 1 EUR = the GBP/EUR rate, and 1 USD = (GBP/EUR) ÷ (USD/EUR). The server fetches new rates on start-up and every 6 hours. The ECB publishes on working days around 16:00 CET; for weekends and holidays the last published rate is used. The rate is filled in automatically on invoices, payments and journals but can always be overwritten with the rate actually achieved.

Chart of accounts & reporting

Every ledger account is mapped to a reporting category (Setup tab), which decides where it appears in the financial statements: fixed assets, trade receivables, other current assets, cash & bank, equity, trade payables and other current liabilities on the balance sheet; revenue, cost of sales, overheads, financial income and financial expenses on the P&L. P&L accounts are not closed off to retained earnings at year end; instead the cumulative result is shown as its own line within equity. The cash flow statement uses the indirect method.

Invoices

Sales and purchase invoices can have any number of lines, each booked to its own ledger account with its own VAT rate. Purchase invoices post Dr expense/asset lines, Dr VAT recoverable, Cr accounts payable; sales invoices post Dr accounts receivable, Cr revenue lines, Cr VAT payable.

Foreign-exchange gain/loss

A foreign-currency invoice is booked to accounts payable/receivable at the rate on the invoice date. When it is settled, from any bank account, the part settled is cleared at that original booked rate, while the bank leg is recorded at what actually went through the bank in GBP (the bank amount times the ECB rate for a foreign-currency account, or simply the GBP amount for the GBP account). The difference is posted to Realised FX gain (financial income) or Realised FX loss (financial expenses). Example: a 1,000 USD invoice booked at 800 GBP and paid from the GBP account for 750 GBP gives a 50 GBP gain.

Period-end revaluation of unrealised FX on open invoices and foreign-currency bank balances is not done yet.

Manual journals & bank statements

Manual journals can post to any account and must balance before they are saved. Bank statements are imported from CAMT files and linked to a bank ledger account (automatically via the IBAN once known). Each line is booked against an opposing account or matched to an open invoice. Note that a manual journal or bank line posted directly to accounts receivable/payable does not mark any invoice as paid; use the invoice option for that.

Cost centres

Cost centres (departments) can be chosen on P&L lines only: invoice lines, manual journal lines, bank postings to a P&L account, and depreciation (taken from the asset). Balance sheet lines never carry one. Automatic FX gains/losses have no cost centre and show as "Unallocated". The Financials tab can show the P&L for one cost centre, and has a P&L per cost centre.

Periods

Every journal belongs to the monthly period of its date. Periods are created automatically when needed. Closing a period first books its depreciation and then blocks all postings dated in it; periods are closed in order and reopened in reverse order. The opening-balance journal is always treated as brought forward in the reports, never as a movement.

Fixed assets & depreciation

Assets are either machinery or inventory (fixtures, fittings & office/IT equipment - Dutch "inventaris", not stock). Each type has its own investment ledger and accumulated depreciation ledger (balance sheet) and depreciation ledger (P&L). Depreciation is straight-line: cost × yearly % ÷ 12 per month (20% = five years), from the month of acquisition until the book value is zero, with no residual value. It is posted as one journal per month, dated the last day, with each asset's cost centre on the expense line. The server checks every 6 hours and books any month that has ended; it can also be run by hand. Assets owned before the books started carry the depreciation already written off as "opening depreciation". Disposals are not modelled yet.

Paying suppliers

The Pay button opens a simulated bank payment window: choose the account to pay from and your bank, "log in" and approve. No real bank is contacted and no money moves; approving books the payment on the invoice as if the bank executed it today. When paying from an account in another currency, the bank converts at today's ECB rate and the difference with the invoice's booked rate is a realised FX gain or loss. Real payments would need an FCA-authorised Open Banking payment initiation provider (e.g. TrueLayer or Yapily), which can later replace the simulated bank behind the same button. A payment made this way would also appear on the next real bank statement; that statement line is then already settled and should be posted against accounts payable or left out.

VAT

Input VAT is posted to VAT recoverable and output VAT to VAT payable. No VAT return, partial exemption or reverse-charge mechanics are modelled; overseas invoices in the demo data use 0% VAT.

Persistence

All data is stored server-side in a SQLite database file, so it survives page refreshes and is visible from any browser hitting this URL.
